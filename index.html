Data Story

Have summer nights near Phoenix Williams Gateway Airport grown warmer since 2016, and does the pattern fit climate change and urban heat?

In this analysis, we will explore the relationship between temperature changes and urban heat islands in the Phoenix Williams Gateway Airport area. In Phoenix, afternoon temperatures can exceed over 100 degrees, but what about at night? When temperatures remain high over time, the urban heat island effect becomes more pronounced at night. EPA reports that hotter days, less night time cools adds to air pollution, health condition, breathing problems, heat stroke, and death. This story will examine hourly weather observations from the Phoenix Williams Gateway Airport comparing the summer of 2016 through 2025. We will determine whether summer nights have grown warmer and whether this might have a correlation with climate change and urban heat. The data points towards yes where the average daily low rose about 1.8 degrees Fahrenheit between the first five summers and the last five summers while daytime high had minimal differences.

D1 · Story Frame

Data used within this data story comes from the NOAA NCEI Local Climatological Database for Phoenix Williams Gateway Airport(WBAN:23104), which covers hourly observations from January 1, 2016 to August 24, 2025. To keep every year comparable, I used June 1st to August 24th for all of time values in the table. The daily highs and lows are the highest and lowest hourly dry-bulb temperatures recorded during each day. A "warm night" was classified as a day with a low of 80 degrees Fahrenheit or higher. Furthermore, an analysis from one airport alone cannot separate climate change from local urban heat effects. The SVG in D2 section was created using Microsoft Excel with the cited data source while the D4 SVG is handcoded.
